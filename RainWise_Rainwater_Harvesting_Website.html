Why harvest rainwater?

Rain that runs off a roof can be collected instead of being wasted. A well-planned system can reduce pressure on mains water and help communities prepare for dry periods.

🏠

Useful at home

Collect rain from a clean roof and store it for suitable household uses.

🌱

Save resources

Reduce demand for treated drinking water for tasks that do not need drinking-quality water.

🌧️

Manage runoff

With correct overflow planning, harvesting can reduce some roof runoff and support groundwater recharge where suitable.

How to collect and store water

A simple system has five parts. The exact design and tank size should suit local rainfall, roof area, demand, space and building rules.

Catch

Keep the roof and gutters clear of leaves, litter and animal waste.

Divert & filter

Use a leaf screen and first-flush diverter to keep the first dirty runoff out of the tank.

Store safely

Use a covered, opaque tank with a secure lid, screened inlet and overflow outlet.

Maintain

Inspect before rainy seasons, clean gutters and filters, and remove sediment from the tank as needed.

What can harvested rainwater be used for?

🌿 Gardening and watering plants
Often a good use for appropriately collected water.
🧹 Outdoor cleaning and washing floors
Use a clean collection system and avoid contact with contaminated water.
🚽 Toilet flushing
Requires suitable plumbing, backflow prevention and local code compliance.
🧺 Laundry or other household uses
Only if water quality and system design are suitable for that use.
🌍 Groundwater recharge
A recharge pit or trench may be suitable after professional site assessment; do not direct contaminated runoff underground.
🥤 Drinking and cooking
Do not drink untreated rainwater. Proper treatment, disinfection and regular testing are essential.
Safety first: Rainwater can contain germs and chemicals from roofs, gutters, air pollution or storage. Keep tanks sealed against insects, separate harvested-water pipes from drinking-water plumbing, and follow local public-health guidance. Never enter a tank.
